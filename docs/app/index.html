The waltz of one place, seen by radar

Pick a cell of NISAR's own orbit grid, open a point and follow everything that changed there since launch: vegetation you could see, and water in the soil and ground stability that only the radar measures.

Where NISAR looks from two sides

Blue frames are dawn passes (ascending), purple frames are evening passes (descending). Where they cross, the ground is seen from two angles: each crossing is a cell, coloured by how much data it has.

    Step 4 · Data

    Only NISAR, four filters

    Every file comes from NASA's catalog. We keep GCOV (radar brightness), GUNW (interferometry) and SME2 (soil moisture), with four filters: period since launch, the file really covers the point, one version per pass (validated over provisional), and the product's own masks.
